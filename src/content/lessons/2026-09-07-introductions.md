## Grammar: minä and minun

**Minä** means “I”. **Minun** means “my” or “of me”.

- **Minä olen Nyan.** — I am Nyan.
- **Minun nimeni on Nyan.** — My name is Nyan.

In standard Finnish, *nimeni* includes the possessive suffix **-ni** (“my”).

## Grammar: where I live

**Minä asun Espoossa.** — I live in Espoo.

The inessive ending **-ssa/-ssä** often means “in”. Vowel harmony determines which ending to use; the word stem can also change.

| Basic form | In a place |
| --- | --- |
| Espoo | Espoossa |
| Helsinki | Helsingissä |
| talo (house) | talossa |

## Phrases

- **Mikä sinun nimesi on?** — What is your name?
- **Missä sinä asut?** — Where do you live?
- **Minä asun Espoossa.** — I live in Espoo.

## Mistakes to remember

- Incorrect for “My name is Nyan”: **Minä nimi on Nyan.**
- Correct: **Minun nimeni on Nyan.**
- Why: use the possessive form when expressing “my name”.
